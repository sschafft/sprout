## Where I'm from

I was born in Bangkok and spent my first six years in [Aranyaprathet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aranyaprathet), a town on the Cambodian border where my parents worked with refugees. We then moved to Vietnam, Romania, and India, where I finished high school in Bangalore.

After a year volunteering in El Salvador, I studied at UBC in Vancouver, then moved to Washington, DC. My parents kept going — Kyrgyzstan, Laos, North Korea — before retiring to Sidney, BC.

I now live in Ann Arbor, Michigan, with my wife Taylor, our son Calvin, and our daughter June. I spend my free time camping and traveling with my family, mountain biking, skiing, as well as trying to stop at least one of our cats from eating plastic toys.

I am appreciative of my upbringing that helped me embrace the complexity in the world around us and I travel with my family to help remind myself that my view of the world is one of many.

## How I got into tech

In Vietnam, digging through our first computer in DOS, I found a ninja game a kind vendor had left on it. That moment of wonder has driven most of my career.

Games kept me learning. I set up networks to play LAN games with my brother, built and overclocked our computers, and was on a first-name basis with the vendors on Chennai's [Ritchie Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ritchie_Street).

I've also erroneously bet against the iPhone and spent hours modding the ROM on an [HTC TyTN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HTC_TyTN_II) instead. Since then: SQL, PHP, Rails, Vue, React, Elixir, Python — and VBA and Drupal, never again.

## Something I enjoy deeply

Taking something complex and making it tractable. I taught English in El Salvador, Kyrgyzstan, and China, later designed training and ran large workshops using adult-learning theory, and now do the same thing with systems: find the shape of the problem, name the parts, and give a team something it can act on.

My best work combines the two: building tools that help people reach their goals. I pick what fits the problem, not what's shiny or familiar.

I learned tech by doing, so I studied what I didn't know: business and organization development. Whether software succeeds usually depends more on people than on code.

## Where I've landed

Much of my career was in international development. It runs in the family — we've lived abroad for nearly 200 years, in India and Iran, even starting a [school in Tamil Nadu](https://maps.app.goo.gl/k2R4PgM7kLrcLJX37).

Lately I've built internal infrastructure at Google, which has been its own anthropological deep dive.

I'm happiest deep in a hard problem with a team that cares about the craft as much as the output.
